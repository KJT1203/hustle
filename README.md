# hustle.

An idle life simulator: `index.html`, `style.css` and `game.js`, no build step. You turn 18, and the clock starts. A full life takes about a day of play.

**Time**: the top bar shows the date and time. On **Auto**, a game day passes in about 5 seconds while you're idle, slows to about 30 seconds while you're clicking or typing, and slows again to trading pace (a stock market session in about 30 seconds) while you're on Markets or Crypto. **Fast** runs a day a second. Time keeps passing while the game is closed, at a day every 15 real minutes with no cap (overnight is about a month, a week about two years), and a summary greets you when you're back. Everything in the economy is priced per game day, so the pace changes how long things take for you, not how much they earn.

- **Life**: health, happiness, smarts and looks. Things to do, education, dating, marriage, kids, a cat.
- **Pacing**: degrees take real time (a bachelor's 3 years, law 3, medicine 4, a PhD 5), gigs are capped at three a day, living costs grow with your pay (rent about a quarter of salary while you rent), and gains in happiness and smarts shrink as they climb. A sharp player reaches $100K around 20-21, $1M in their mid-20s to 30s, and $10M by their mid-40s.
- **Education**: a diploma, bachelor's, master's, MBA, law school, medical school and a PhD (which pays a stipend), plus a trade certificate, police academy, coding bootcamp, flight school and teaching certificate. There are five schools, from an online university you can study at around a job to an elite university that is hard to get into and impresses interviewers. Majors count as a year of experience, and you can win a scholarship. While studying you have a grade, and can study hard, party or hire a tutor. Student loans repay themselves daily. Lawyers need a JD, surgeons an MD, pilots flight school.
- **People**: a family with parents and siblings, plus friends, partners, exes and named kids who grow up and move out. Each person has a closeness score you raise by calling, hanging out, gifts and date nights. You can propose, try for a baby, break up or divorce. Closeness fades if you neglect people, a neglected partner can leave, parents grow old and leave an inheritance, and your mood follows how close you are to them.
- **Choices**: random decisions show up in your inbox (lost wallets, crypto pitches, bosses, scandals, buyouts). If you ignore one for 30 days, it decides for you.
- **Work**: 19 jobs across 13 fields, including chef, electrician, police, teacher, designer, pilot and CEO. Experience builds in the field you work in, and better jobs require it. Applying means an interview where your answer changes your odds. A performance score (work hard, slack off, network, ask for a raise) decides promotions, and each rank asks for more. Drop below 20 and you can be fired. Retire on a pension from 55.
- **Businesses**: 9 tiers from a lemonade stand to a rocket company. Costs are real-world (a food truck $45K, a coffee shop $180K, a car wash $900K) and each milestone adds 50% to a business's income; each extra unit costs more than the last, steeply for lemonade stands. A manager costs twice a unit's price and runs every unit of that business. Returns run from about 50% a year for a food truck to 12-16% for hotels, banks and rockets. Profits follow the economy through each business's sensitivity and fixed costs (a hotel can lose money in a deep recession), and competition closes a location now and then.
- **Economy**: an output gap that follows the market cycle with a lag drives unemployment, inflation and recessions (about 15 a century, around a year each). A central bank sets rates with a Taylor rule in quarter-point steps every six weeks; mortgages lock in the going rate. Prices, wages, tuition and business revenue rise with inflation, recessions bring layoffs and tougher interviews, and house prices follow inflation, growth and mortgage rates. Home shows the state of the economy.
- **Stocks**: a Webull/moomoo-style trading screen with 110 stocks: 8 made-up companies, the world's biggest names lightly renamed (Nvibia, Microsaft, Appel, Alphabut, Amazin, Tessla, Birkshire Hathaway, Eli Lolly, Koka-Kola and more), and 52 penny stocks that pump, dump and go bankrupt below a cent.
  - *How it moves*: returns follow each stock's beta (CAPM) and its real-world volatility, with market-wide, sector and company-specific parts. Market volatility clusters and jumps when prices fall (GJR-GARCH), shown as a Fear index. Bull markets average about 4½ years and bear markets about one, and big companies face competition that pulls their size back toward normal. Over a century the Hustle 500 (cap-weighted, including 442 unlisted companies that move with the market) returns about 7% a year in price, with about 20% volatility, fat tails, a quarter of years down, and occasional crashes and deep bear markets.
  - *The exchange*: a real calendar with exchange holidays and 1 PM early closes, trading 9:30 to 4:00 with the busiest trading at the open and close, overnight gaps, and circuit breakers (15-minute halts at −7% and −13%, closed for the day at −20%). Corrections and bear markets are called from the index's drawdown.
  - *Companies*: quarterly earnings against an estimate that move the stock at the open, dividends paid on each stock's own ex-dividend dates (the price drops by the payout), stock splits above $1,000, a profile, estimated financials and a shareholder list.
  - *Trading*: market, limit and stop orders, good for the day or until cancelled; take profit, stop loss and trailing stops (5/10/20% under the high since you set them) on any position (stocks and crypto), set by price or with quick percentage buttons, or attached to a limit or stop buy so they switch on when it fills, where whichever is hit first sells the whole position and cancels the other (a stop that the price gaps through fills at the gapped price); bid and ask prices with a spread that widens for small stocks and in panics; price impact that grows with the square root of order size against daily volume. Margin: borrow up to half a purchase at the policy rate plus 3%; fall below 25% equity (30% on shorts) and the broker sells for you, and an underwater account leaves you owing the difference. Short selling: borrow shares to sell, buy them back to cover, with 50% initial equity (100% for penny stocks, which can't be shorted under $1), a daily borrow fee from 0.3% to 100% a year, dividends owed while short, and short-term tax on the gain. Options: buy calls and puts on the 58 main stocks for the next three monthly expiries (third Fridays), priced with Black-Scholes from each stock's live volatility, with a skew toward low strikes and upcoming earnings priced in; 100 shares a contract, a 3% spread, sell to close any time the market is open, and in-the-money options cash-settle at the close on expiry. Own more than half a company and you control it: its profits pay you daily, and you can invest in growth, pay a special dividend or rename it.
- **Bank**: a savings account at the central bank's rate less half a point; the Hustle 500 index fund (the whole market in one purchase, 0.03% fee, about 1.5% dividends a year); 1-year bills and 10-year notes at the going yields, whose prices fall when rates rise if you sell early; and a retirement account in the index fund, fed automatically from your pay with a 50% employer match up to 6%, capped yearly, with a 10% penalty for taking money out before 60.
- **Taxes**: progressive income tax (10-37% with a standard deduction, brackets indexed to inflation) on pay, business profit, interest, gigs, severance and net casino winnings; capital gains taxed like income if held a year or less and at 0/15/20% after that, with losses offsetting gains and up to $3,000 of income; dividends at the lower rate; half of rent sheltered; the first $250K of gain on the home you live in tax-free; 1% property tax. Retirement contributions come off taxable income. Tax is settled as you earn, and Life shows the year so far.
- **Crypto**: its own boom-and-bust market that never closes, with prices moving every minute, nights and weekends included, with 6 coins and a $1 stablecoin that pays interest. You buy with dollar amounts and pay a 1% fee on every trade. New meme coins launch every few weeks, and most get rugged.
- **Casino**: a lobby of five games showing each house edge and your record. Open a game to play it: blackjack, single-zero roulette with a full board, dice (under/over/exactly 7), coin flip, and slots. Bets run from $10 to $1M. Chase your losses and you will get asked to ban yourself.
- **Chirp**: a simulated social feed. Post selfies, hot takes, memes and flexes, go viral, get ratio'd, and earn from sponsorships.
- **Property**: a housing market that rises and falls, listings across 8 neighbourhoods, 30-year mortgages with 20% down, and tenants. Live in one place to stop paying rent and rent the rest out, up to office towers and a private island.
- **Garage**: a dealership from a used hatchback to a vintage racer. Most cars lose value, while collectibles swing in price and tend to climb. Your best car lifts your mood, and every car brings followers.
- **Lifestyle**: gadgets, art, yachts and jets for mood and followers, each with upkeep.
- **Leaderboard** (published page only): each player's family posts its name, generation, net worth and goals to a shared board shown on Home, at most once a minute when something changes; names come from the viewer's Claude profile at render time. Hide my family removes your row. Off the published page (a file or the installed app) the section simply doesn't appear.
- **Generations**: when you die, you pick which of your kids takes over. They start at their real age (or 18 if younger), keep your surviving spouse as a parent and your other kids as siblings, and get a little of your smarts and looks. Closer kids start happier. The heir inherits half your net worth plus a permanent business bonus. With no kids, a relative inherits.
- **Goals**: 28 milestones, from opening your first business to a billion-dollar net worth. The next three show on Home, and the full list is behind See all. Goals are a family trophy case that carries across generations.

**Playing it:** a short guided tour starts with every new life (reopen it with the ? button or the ? key). the Home screen lists everything that needs you. Answer decisions right there, collect full tills, and hire managers when they pay off. Quick actions sit underneath, with your net worth chart and a breakdown of where your money is. The side menu groups the other screens and flags what needs attention; on a phone it becomes a bottom tab bar. Keys 1–0 switch screens, Space pauses, C collects everything.

Play: open `index.html` (keep the files together). It saves to the browser automatically; use **Back up your save** in the ? menu to keep a copy or move it to another device.

Install as an app: serve the folder over https (for example GitHub Pages) or from `localhost` (`python -m http.server` in the folder, then open http://localhost:8000), and use the install button in Chrome or Edge's address bar. It then opens in its own window and plays offline (`manifest.webmanifest`, `sw.js`, `icon-*.png`).
Self-check: open with `?test=1` (a query string, so serve the file with a server that keeps queries) and look for `self-test passed` in the console.
